## HERO

### Stablecoin regulation: compare legal status, not just policy direction

Jurisdictions increasingly discuss the same themes — reserves, redemption, governance and AML/CFT — but similar policy language does not mean that their regimes are at the same legal stage.

This page separates:
- rules already in force;
- enacted laws not yet effective;
- final rules with future commencement;
- implementation rulemaking;
- consultations;
- restrictive/prohibitive boundaries.

**Last verified: 16 October 2026**

This page is for comparative research and is not legal advice.

## Status logic

`Law enacted ≠ regime in force`  
`Framework finalised ≠ binding issuer regime`  
`Licence granted ≠ token publicly launched`

## Current jurisdiction status

### United States
**Enacted — implementation rulemaking — not yet effective**

The GENIUS Act was enacted on 18 July 2025. Its delayed effective date is the earlier of 18 months after enactment or 120 days after the primary federal regulators issue final implementing regulations. Federal implementation rulemaking remains underway as of 16 October 2026; official materials currently expect 18 January 2027 absent an earlier trigger.

The statute establishes, among other things:
- permitted payment stablecoin issuer pathways;
- at least 1:1 identifiable eligible reserves;
- public redemption policies;
- monthly reserve-composition disclosure;
- independent accounting examination/certification;
- capital, liquidity, risk-management and AML/CFT implementation requirements.

Do not label the federal regime as already fully in force.

### European Union
**MiCA in force / applicable**

MiCA regulates asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) and e-money tokens (EMTs), with authorisation, reserve, governance, risk-management, disclosure and supervisory requirements.

ART/EMT provisions have applied since 30 June 2024.

The 2026 MiCA review does not suspend the current regime.

### United Kingdom
**Final rules published — full commencement 25 October 2027**

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 were made on 4 February 2026.

The FCA published final stablecoin-issuance rules on 30 June 2026. The full cryptoasset regime commences on 25 October 2027.

Systemic stablecoins are subject to a distinct Bank of England / FCA joint-regulation layer.

### Hong Kong
**Issuer regime in force — licensed issuers exist**

The Stablecoins Ordinance issuer regime took effect on 1 August 2025.

The HKMA register currently lists two licensed issuers with licences effective on 10 April 2026.

Requirements cover licensing, high-quality/liquid reserves, segregation, par redemption, governance, technology and AML/CFT.

Licence status does not by itself prove that the related token is already broadly/publicly launched.

### Singapore
**Implementation consultation — dedicated MAS-SCS regime not yet in force**

MAS finalised the policy design in 2023.

On 1 September 2026, MAS opened consultation on legislative amendments to the Payment Services Act to implement the MAS-SCS framework; the consultation closes on 16 October 2026.

The planned framework covers Singapore-issued SCS pegged to SGD or G10 currencies, with reserve, capital, par-redemption and disclosure safeguards.

Do not describe existing DPT/MPI activity as issuance under a live MAS-SCS regime.

### Japan
**Electronic Payment Instrument regime in force**

Japan's electronic-payment-instrument framework has operated since 1 June 2023.

Relevant intermediaries require registration, and a new electronic-payment-instrument / cryptoasset service intermediary regime began on 1 June 2026.

Use the statutory category `Electronic Payment Instruments`; do not invent a single generic “stablecoin licence”.

### UAE — CBUAE onshore perimeter
**Payment Token Services Regulation in force**

CBUAE Regulation C 2/2024 has been in force since 31 August 2024.

It covers payment-token issuance, custody/transfer and conversion, with separate treatment for foreign issuers.

Requirements include reserve protection/segregation, face-value coverage, par redemption, capital and audit requirements.

This row does not represent the whole UAE: VARA, ADGM and DIFC are separate regulatory perimeters.

### Mainland China
**Restrictive / prohibitive boundary**

The current baseline is the eight-agency Notice 银发〔2026〕42号 dated 6 February 2026, which replaced the 2021 notice.

Virtual-currency-related business remains illegal financial activity within Mainland China. The notice expressly addresses stablecoins, offshore RMB-linked issuance and offshore issuance by Mainland entities and their controlled overseas entities.

This is not an issuer-licensing regime comparable with Hong Kong, the EU or the future US framework.

## Cross-jurisdiction convergence

Common themes:
- issuer authorisation;
- reserve quality and segregation;
- par/timely redemption;
- disclosure and assurance;
- AML/CFT;
- operational resilience.

## Key divergence

Still materially different:
- legal classification;
- eligible issuer types;
- treatment of foreign stablecoins;
- systemic-supervision layer;
- holder-identity/distribution requirements;
- treatment of interest or yield.

## International baselines

### FSB
2023 recommendations remain an important global baseline. Current 2026 work focuses increasingly on cross-border vulnerabilities and financial-stability monitoring.

### FATF
The 2026 targeted report focuses on illicit-finance risks involving stablecoins and P2P activity through unhosted wallets.

### CPMI–IOSCO
PFMI guidance applies to stablecoin arrangements that perform a transfer function and are determined to be systemically important.

These standards coordinate regulatory outcomes; they do not replace domestic law.
